ESP32-Mega & Proto Shield Setup

Phase 2: CRITICAL Electrical Differences

This is the most important section. Because you are using an ESP32 rather than an ATmega2560 chip, the electrical rules change. This dashboard highlights the critical voltage thresholds you must respect to avoid permanent hardware damage.

⚠ 3.3V Logic Danger

A standard Arduino Mega uses 5V logic. The ESP32 uses 3.3V logic.

If you solder a sensor to your Proto Shield that outputs a 5V signal back to the ESP32, you will permanently damage the ESP32 chip.

  • Always power sensors from the 3.3V pin, not the 5V pin.
  • If a sensor requires 5V, you MUST use a Logic Level Converter on the signal line.

Maximum Safe Logic Voltage

The 5V Pin

The 5V pin on the Proto Shield will still output 5V (drawn from USB/VIN). This is fine for powering relays or motors directly, but never connect this 5V power directly to the ESP32's digital or analog input pins.

Pin Mapping

Pay close attention to the silkscreen on your board. The physical location of "Pin 13" on a standard Mega might map to a different GPIO number (e.g., GPIO 18) on your ESP32. You must use the ESP32 GPIO numbers in your code.

Phase 3: Wiring a Test Circuit (LED)

Let's build a simple circuit on the Proto Shield's central area to test your setup. Follow the interactive steps below to place components and route wires logically before writing the code.

1. Place an LED

Insert an LED into the central prototyping area of the shield.

2. Ground Connection

Connect the short leg (cathode) of the LED to a 220 ohm resistor, then to a GND rail.

3. Signal Connection

Connect the long leg (anode) of the LED to a digital pin (e.g., GPIO 13).
